NTH DESKTOP 3D
Video review and implementation brief
Prepared November 11, 2026

STATUS

This is the design and engineering brief for a new Android home launcher.
It records the supplied video references, proposed modes, and acceptance
criteria. No Android source, APK, device performance measurements, or
repository changes have been produced in this pass. Mode names below are
proposed Nth Desktop names, not verified names of the reference products.

PRODUCT DIRECTION

Create a launcher whose desktop, icons, menus, folders, flyouts, and built-in
windows share a coherent sense of space. Phone tilt changes the viewing
angle and separates background layers. Touch remains the primary control.

Use one launcher with independently selectable background scenes, desktop
layouts, transitions, and menu styles. Presets offer complete looks without
preventing combinations such as a Living Pond background with a rotating
Start menu. Switching presets must preserve shortcuts and folders.

The first install should demonstrate a tasteful amount of depth immediately.
Use Spatial Desktop as the proposed default, with an original layered
landscape, a stable bottom taskbar, and a lifting Start menu. Provide an
obvious mode chooser and a short tip about tilting the phone.

PROPOSED MODES

1. SPATIAL DESKTOP

  Main reference: 226449.mp4 at 00:50–01:20, with transition ideas from
  226446.mp4 at 00:28–00:46.

  Home pages become panels with thickness, perspective, and shadows.
  Swiping can rotate a cube, turn a panel like a hinged door, move through
  a curved carousel, or tilt a stack of cards. Cube and carousel are our
  proposed selectable implementations, not verified effect names.

  Icons rise slightly when pressed. Folders lift forward and open into
  a fan or compact spread. Built-in windows can join a depth-stack switcher.

  Keep taskbar controls anchored at the bottom. Rotating home pages must
  not move the Start button, clock, or tray out of reach. Desktop content
  reserves space for the taskbar in portrait and landscape.

2. GRAVITY PLAYGROUND

  Main reference: 226449.mp4 at 00:02–00:44.

  Represent selected shortcuts as shallow 3D tiles or discs. Tilting the
  phone changes a simulated gravity vector; objects roll, collide, and
  settle inside a bounded play area. Optional photo tiles can join them.

  Pinning holds important shortcuts in place. A visible Arrange action
  returns items to an orderly layout. Hold an object to steady it and open
  its context menu. Capture the selected item's stable identifier so
  movement cannot cause the wrong app to launch.

  Begin with shortcuts and still photos. Multiple simultaneous video
  tiles need a separate performance experiment before being included.
  Physics remains confined to the play area; taskbar controls stay fixed.

3. HOLOGRAPHIC DEPTH

  Main reference: 226446.mp4 at 01:04–02:15.

  Use separate background, middle, subject, and foreground layers. Their
  differing movement as the viewing angle changes creates depth. Add
  optional particles, lighting, and subtle icon shadows.

  Ship original scenes such as a starfield, underwater setting, and
  abstract landscape. Existing comic characters in the recording serve
  only as references; they are not bundled artwork for the new app.

  Support a single-photo mode with modest whole-image movement, plus
  a richer layered-image mode. A single photograph must not be described
  as automatically containing separated depth layers. Automatic depth
  estimation would be an additional feature with its own investigation.

4. LIVING POND

  Main reference: 226451.mp4 at 00:16–00:42.

  Give the scene separate pebble, fish, water, and floating-leaf depths.
  Fish follow smooth paths, leaves drift, and background touches produce
  ripples. Add a gentle tilt-driven view shift and moving shadows.

  Expose background selection, fish count, fish speed, ripples, and shadows.
  Our implementation can allow ripples and shadows together rather than
  copying the reference's either/or choice.

  Water response must not intercept taps intended for shortcuts, menus,
  widgets, or the taskbar. Let animation run only while home is visible.

3D THROUGHOUT THE LAUNCHER

  Start menu: Choose a lifting layered panel, rotating category carousel,
  or unfolding book. Keep app names readable and the focused selection
  facing the user. Search text and keyboard input use native Android UI.

  Hold/right-click menus: A compact panel unfolds from the selected item.
  Include relevant actions such as Open, Add to desktop, Pin/Unpin,
  Rename shortcut, App info, and Uninstall through Android's own flow.
  Available actions depend on whether the item is an app, folder,
  shortcut, or built-in feature.

  Flyouts: Calendar, sound, and device controls swing or lift from their
  taskbar anchor, then settle into a readable, touch-friendly position.
  Keep Android-controlled actions in their supported system screens.

  Folders: A folder can open like a box, spread into a fan, or expand into
  a floating grid. Long lists must remain searchable and practical.

  Built-in windows: Add restrained thickness, lighting, shadows, opening
  and closing motion, and a stack/carousel switcher. Resizing and dragging
  continue to work after the animation settles.

  Further experiments: A room with app shelves; a radial shortcut wheel;
  a floating clock with flip digits; a portal-like app launch animation;
  and an optional reflective desktop floor. Treat these as later options
  after the main four modes work reliably.

  Native Android apps still launch through Android. A launcher can style
  its own home scene and built-in content, but should not promise arbitrary
  3D control of another app's interface or unrestricted live app previews.

ENGINEERING FOUNDATION

  Use a new shared scene/rendering layer with native Android launcher
  services. A practical initial candidate is Kotlin with an OpenGL ES
  scene for perspective, depth, textured geometry, lighting, and picking.
  Check the required graphics capabilities at runtime or declare the
  minimum accurately. A 3D scene needs more than a wallpaper animation.

  Separate scene data, animation state, and Android actions. App identity
  uses component plus user/profile, not the visible label or icon position.
  Use Android's LauncherApps interface for app entries and launching.

  Reuse appropriate existing Nth logic for folders, setup, app discovery,
  backup, and built-in utilities once the source baseline is selected.
  The new renderer should not require duplicating each utility for every
  mode. Keep editable text, accessibility focus, and ordinary controls
  available through native Android surfaces.

  Host external widgets through AppWidgetHost. Prototype the combination
  of native interactive widgets and scene transitions early; rendering a
  preview texture is not equivalent to hosting a live interactive widget.
  The proposed approach is to animate a representation during a page
  transition and restore the real host view when the page settles.

  Keep scene selection, transition style, menu style, motion intensity,
  and quality independent in saved settings. Maintain one canonical layout
  so changing an effect cannot lose an icon or create duplicate shortcuts.

  Plan .nthdesktop compatibility by inspecting the existing schema before
  implementation. Preserve 3D-specific settings as versioned fields and
  map imported ordinary layouts into a safe 3D default. Compatibility has
  not yet been implemented or verified for this new launcher.

MOTION AND INPUT

  Prefer the game rotation vector where available for smooth relative
  orientation. Fall back to gravity or a filtered accelerometer. Detect
  sensor availability rather than assuming a specific phone configuration.

  Establish a neutral pose when home opens, support Recenter, remap axes
  for screen orientation, smooth the signal, and clamp its effect. Start
  with a modest camera angle; expose stronger motion as an option.
  Avoid depending on compass direction or unbounded accumulated yaw.

  Start/stop sensor listeners and rendering with visible lifecycle state.
  Pause when home is covered or the screen is off. Reduce ambient motion
  and rendering cost under battery saver. Do not add a background sensor
  service solely to keep a hidden home scene moving.

  Keep menus and touch targets steady while being used. Convert touch
  coordinates with the same current transforms used to render an object.
  Resolve the nearest visible hit, respect clipping, and prevent back-face
  or hidden icons from receiving taps.

  Offer motion strength, animation speed, tilt on/off, reduced motion,
  and a simple stable layout. Effects must have a usable touch-only path.

FIRST BUILD AND SUBSEQUENT PASSES

  First Android build: a real HOME launcher, installed apps and search,
  persistent shortcuts/folders, a stable taskbar, one original layered
  background with tilt, cube/carousel page transitions, a lifting 3D Start
  menu, a hold menu, basic flyouts, and a mode/settings preview.

  Next pass: selectable Gravity Playground and Living Pond scenes,
  additional holographic backgrounds, folder styles, and menu variants.

  Following pass: integrate selected Nth utilities and verify their window
  behavior, external widget hosting, backup imports, performance, and
  accessibility. Room/shelf and heavier shader experiments follow evidence
  that the core launcher remains smooth and comfortable.

  This order is a proposed implementation sequence, not a claim that any
  milestone has already been completed. The new repository URL/source
  baseline is still needed before a repository-backed Android build.

ACCEPTANCE CHECKS

  - First launch offers the default-home flow and explains tilt briefly.
  - App launch, return Home, app installation/removal, and profile handling
    keep the expected entries and current page consistent.
  - Every mode preserves folders, shortcuts, and taskbar placement.
  - Tap/hold/swipe/drag select the intended object at maximum motion.
  - Start, search, folders, context menus, and flyouts support Back and
    remain usable with the keyboard open and after rotation.
  - No desktop labels or menus disappear beneath the taskbar or cutout.
  - Missing sensors and reduced motion leave a fully usable launcher.
  - Home stops sensor/render work while another app covers it.
  - GPU context recreation restores textures and the selected scene.
  - Check small and large app collections and imported layouts.
  - Profile real frame times, memory, heat, and battery on the Pixel 10.
    Aim for steady 60 fps first; offer a higher refresh option only after
    measurements support it. No device speed or battery claim is made yet.
